# Bank and General Ledger Reconciliations Need Regular Attention

Keeping financial records accurate is not only a bookkeeping task. It supports clearer reporting, better planning, and more reliable business decisions. When bank activity and the general ledger do not agree, it becomes harder to understand the actual financial position of an enterprise.

---

## What is Reconciliation?

Reconciliation means comparing recorded transactions with the corresponding bank or ledger information. The core objective is to identify differences, investigate their underlying causes, and apply appropriate corrections. 

Common causes of discrepancies include:
* **Outstanding transactions** (e.g., uncashed checks, pending deposits)
* **Timing differences** between bank processing and internal recording
* **Missing entries** or unrecorded fees/interest
* **Duplicate records**
* **Posting or data entry errors**

For businesses managing payroll, tax, accounts payable (AP), accounts receivable (AR), and general financial reporting, reconciliation connects several accounting workflows. A single unresolved variance can propagate through financial statements, creating substantial cleanup work later—especially when accurate numbers are urgently needed for audits or executive planning.

---

## Core Reconciliation Pillars

### 1. Bank Reconciliations
Bank reconciliations focus on aligning recorded internal cash activity against statements issued by financial institutions.

### 2. General Ledger (GL) Reconciliations
General ledger reconciliations involve verifying individual balance sheet and income statement account balances, ensuring supporting sub-ledgers and entries are substantiated and mathematically sound.

Together, these processes support:
* Reliable financial reporting
* Timely tax preparation and compliance
* Robust internal controls and fraud prevention
* Strategic forecasting and FP&A activities

## Why Regular Cadence Matters

> **Key Takeaway:** Reconciliation is most effective when managed as a continuous, routine accounting procedure rather than a stressful, year-end cleanup project.

Reviewing records consistently yields several operational benefits:
1. **Faster Resolution:** Discrepancies are caught while transaction details and receipts are still fresh.
2. **Reliable Executive Reporting:** Management reports do not require disclaimers or subsequent restatements.
3. **Audit Readiness:** Supporting records remain organized, verifiable, and compliant.

> In practice, reconciliation is not finished simply because two figures appear to match. The supporting entries must make operational sense—accurate records depend equally on the bottom-line balance and the explanation behind it.
