# A Practical Way to Bring Overdue Invoices Under Control

Late customer payments affect more than the accounts receivable balance. They can make cash planning harder, delay supplier payments, and leave business owners unsure about which invoices need attention first. 

The problem is often not one difficult customer—it is a process that lacks clear ownership, consistent follow-up, and useful reporting.

Collections work also needs to protect customer relationships. A payment reminder should be firm, accurate, and appropriate to the history of the account. If different team members contact the same customer with inconsistent information, the process becomes harder to manage and trust can suffer.

---

## Why a Clear Collections Process Matters

A useful collections process starts with reliable information. The team needs to know:
* Which invoices are overdue
* When they were originally due
* Whether there is an active dispute
* What contact has already taken place

Without that context, follow-up quickly turns into a series of disconnected emails and phone calls.

## What to Review Before Changing the Process

Before introducing new workflows or software, review your current accounts receivable framework:

### 1. Analyze the Current Accounts Receivable (A/R) Aging
Start by reviewing the A/R aging report. Group open invoices by:
* Age (e.g., 30, 60, 90+ days)
* Customer account
* Balance amount
* Identified reason for delay

This breakdown reveals whether overdue balances are concentrated in a few key accounts or distributed across the entire customer base.

### 2. Audit Cross-Department Handoffs
Examine the handoff between sales, operations, billing, and finance. Collections issues frequently originate before an invoice is ever generated:
* Ambiguous contract terms
* Incomplete or missing customer details
* Incorrect billing contacts
* Delays in internal work approvals

### 3. Establish a Regular Review Routine
Management reporting should go beyond historic logging to highlight:
* Open balances and aging trends
* Recent collections outreach and customer responses
* Disputed invoices requiring internal resolution
* Broken payment commitments

The goal is not simply to record what happened, but to determine which accounts need immediate action and assign clear ownership.

## Summary Checklist: Core Rules for Collections

- [ ] **Issue accurate invoices promptly:** Ensure billing details and PO numbers are correct prior to delivery.
- [ ] **Record every touchpoint:** Maintain shared notes on all customer communications.
- [ ] **Isolate disputes early:** Separate legitimate billing disputes from standard late payments.
- [ ] **Assign a clear next action:** Never leave an overdue balance without an assigned owner, action, and target follow-up date.
